HOW TO PLAY CHRONO TRAVELERS
===========================

THE SITUATION
  Project Meridian's temporal tunnel tore a standing rupture and "frayed"
  the downstream timeline. You were on the gantry. Now you're a Chrono
  Traveler, adrift somewhere between 2000 and 5000 A.D., riding Tachyon surges
  and unable to steer. The surface team is still looking; it can't pull
  you back.

WHAT YOU'RE TRYING TO DO
  Push as deep downstream (as far toward 5000 A.D.) as you can, and level
  up doing it. Both are tracked on the leaderboards against every other
  Traveler, past and present.

THE TURN
  Every command you type advances the world one tick: Tachyons drain a
  little, monsters drift, other Travelers act, and years you've visited
  keep simulating whether you're there or not. The world also ticks on
  its own every couple of seconds even while you're just sitting at the
  prompt deciding what to do next - don't linger somewhere with company
  you don't trust.

MOVING & LOOKING
  n / s / e / w        move one room
  look                 redescribe the room you're in (who's here, what's
                       on the floor, what's stirring next door)
  look <dir>           peek into the next room without stepping in
  monsters             list every monster in the year, with its position

TACHYONS - your one resource
  Tachyons cover survival (a slow passive drain; at 0 it costs HP), healing
  (the "heal" command, 3 HP per Tachyon, any time), class abilities, and time
  travel. You top them up almost entirely by "convert"ing loot into Tachyons.
    convert <item> / con <item>   break an item down into Tachyons
  Your Tachyon pool has NO cap - convert a pile of junk and stockpile for a
  long jump; nothing is ever wasted.

FIGHTING
  fight <name>          engage a monster in your room (attack / a also work)
  in a fight, type      attack  - swing
                        cast <ability>  - spend Tachyons on a class ability
                        use <item>  - drink/eat a consumable (same effect as
                        outside combat) instead of attacking that round
  Loot from a kill falls on the floor where it died - nothing lands in
  your pack automatically. Pick it up with  take <item>  or  take all.
  shoot <dir> / point <dir>   fire a readied ranged weapon one room away

  PvP: if there's no monster in your room but a living NPC Traveler is
  standing there, "fight <name>" targets them instead. NPCs are full
  Travelers (docs/GDD.md's scope for PvP without netcode), so the fight
  resolves instantly - no round-by-round prompts - win or lose. Beat one
  and everything they were carrying, equipped gear included, drops on the
  floor for you to take; lose and you get the same death & recall as
  losing to a monster.

ABILITIES OUTSIDE A FIGHT
  Most class abilities only do something mid-fight ("cast <ability>" while
  fighting). A couple work the other way - use  cast <ability>  any time,
  not just in combat:
    Engineer's Jump Rig    teleports you to a random room up to 3 exits
                            away - an escape or a shortcut, not a walk.
    Doctor's Crash Cart     heals the most-wounded living NPC Traveler
                            standing in your room, if one's below 25% HP.
  Spy's Black Market Contacts needs no casting at all - once a Spy hits
  level 25 it's just always-on better store prices.

MONSTERS
  Monsters ignore you until you provoke them - mostly by stepping onto
  their tile again and again, lingering, or shooting them. That builds an
  aggro meter: calm -> alert (it shadows you) -> hostile (it lands one
  ambush hit, but only on an idle turn, never in a store room). Move a
  couple of rooms off and it calms back down.
  A few years hold an APEX ("Frayed <species>") - far tougher, far better
  loot, and almost impossible to provoke. It's an optional fight: take it
  on for the reward, or walk past. Name it to fight it.
  Monsters scavenge the floor only when they need Tachyon fuel or a weapon
  better than they're carrying - otherwise they step over loot.

THE TIME SHARD
  One per year, lying on the floor somewhere. A legendary melee weapon
  that hits 25% harder than the best weapon that year has to offer, and
  sells for a price that scales with how far downstream you found it.
  Monsters and other Travelers can't touch it - it's yours to claim.

MERIDIAN SERUMS
  Two per year on the floor - half as rare as the Time Shard, and like it,
  monsters and Travelers leave them alone. "use" one and pick which stat
  to raise (Strength / Agility / Resolve / Intellect) - it permanently
  adds 5, no timer, it just sticks. Worth going out of your way for.

TRAVELLING
  travel <year>        jump anywhere from 2000 to 5000 A.D.
  travel +N / -N       jump N years forward / back
  travel next / prev   jump to the next / previous Warden year
  Cost is about 1 Tachyon per 25 years, paid on arrival. Later years are
  richer and far deadlier; the game warns you before an "overreach" jump
  but never blocks it - a raid on a year above your level can pay off big.

STORES
  Every year has a supply depot. Sell gear/consumables there for Credits,
  buy staples (heals, boosters, a weapon, armour, a ranged option). No
  store buys junk, ever - "sell all" converts every junk item in your
  pack into Tachyons instead (works anywhere, not just at a store). You
  can also buy an empty storefront and run it - it earns Credits from NPC
  shoppers while you're away.

  Melee weapons and armour wear down with use - fight enough and they'll
  contribute less, and eventually nothing, until you "repair <item>" at
  any store. Cost scales with how worn the piece is; ranged weapons don't
  wear down (they run out of ammo instead).

WARDENS
  Every 50-100 years a Warden stands watch over a pre-collapse tech cache
  - an automated construct with a guaranteed Legendary trophy. It gates
  nothing; beat it when you're ready. "travel next" hops between them.

STAYING ALIVE
  - Convert junk early and often; keep an Tachyon buffer for healing.
  - Don't pace back and forth on a monster's tile unless you mean to
    fight it.
  - A store room is a safe haven - shop and heal there.
  - Overreaching downstream is a gamble, not a mistake. Grab better gear
    and jump back if it gets hot.

Type  help  in game for the full command list.
